Hour 1 · Part A · 20 min

2What IS AI?

AI is software trained on large amounts of example data to recognize patterns and generate outputs — text, images, translations, predictions.

It does not "understand" in a human sense — it produces statistically likely outputs based on its training data.

A calculator follows fixed rules; AI learns patterns from examples — that's why it handles messy real-world things like photos and speech, and also why it can be wrong.

Where it goes wrong: biased data, outdated data, no local context.

📚📷🎤📊 Examples in
⬇️
🔍 Pattern found
⬇️
💬🖼️ Output out
⚠️ …only as good as the examples

A Reusable Way to Explain It

4The Chef Analogy 🍳

A simple way to think about it

"A chef gets good by tasting thousands of dishes. AI 'tastes' thousands of examples, then cooks up something new."

If the chef only ever tasted one region's food, would they cook a good Onam sadya? AI trained on the wrong examples cooks the wrong dish.

Hour 1 · Part B · 15 min

5The Balakrishi Seed Analogy 🌾

A farmer needs many seasons of good data, not one seed, for a reliable harvest.

AI needs many, good-quality examples to learn from — that learning phase is called training.

Your filter for ANY tool: "What was this trained on — and is it appropriate for Malayalam, for Kerala, for the work I do?"

🌱 Data → 🌿 Training → 🧪 Test → 🌾 Result
⤴ Your job: check the seed.
Your Vetting Responsibility

6Garbage In, Garbage Out

A tool trained on biased or inappropriate data will give biased or inappropriate answers.

Checking a tool before relying on it is part of using AI responsibly — the same instinct as double-checking any new source of information.

A real example

AI image tools trained mostly on Western faces struggle with Malayali features — that's "the wrong seed," not magic failure.

The Specific Rules

What Never Goes Into an AI Tool

🚫

Never upload/share

• Children's full names or photos with faces
• Addresses, school names, phone numbers
• Any child's personal story or health detail
• Login credentials

✅

Always do instead

• Use your own adult account
• Describe situations generically: "a child in my unit…"
• Screenshot outputs you want to keep, then close the app
• When unsure, ask your mentor first

A simple rule that covers you either way

If a task never requires typing a child's personal details or uploading their photo, you're almost always on safe ground.

Know the Basics

Children's Data Protection — What to Know

India has a growing legal framework specifically covering how personal data — including children's data — can be collected and used. You don't need to be a lawyer, but you do need to know the shape of it.

⚖️

The general principle

A minor's personal data generally needs a parent or guardian's consent before it can be collected by any digital service — this is the direction Indian data-protection law is moving in.

📞

What this means for you

Never create individual accounts for children on AI tools. If in doubt about a specific tool or situation, check with your mentor or CDS — guidance is updated periodically.

Preparedness, Not Panic

If Something Goes Wrong 🚨

1️⃣

A tool gave harmful/wrong output

Stop using it for that task. Report it to your mentor — one report protects many units.

2️⃣

Data was shared by mistake

Delete it if possible, tell your mentor immediately, inform the parent. Honesty fast beats silence.

3️⃣

You're just unsure

"I'll check and come back to you" is a professional answer, not a failure.

Remember

Escalating is success, not failure. Reporting a problem protects every unit in the network.
